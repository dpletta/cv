Diagnosis-Agnostic Sequence Mining to Detect Bipolar-Pattern Suicide Risk and Structural Inequities in Adolescents and Young Adults2026-2028
Role
Principal Investigator
Funder
National Institute on Minority Health and Health Disparities (NIMHD), National Institutes of Health (NIH)
Mechanism
Loan Repayment Program (L70), Facilitating Opportunities in Cutting-edge Science (FOCUS)
Award No.
1L70MD021651-01
Total Costs
$31,013
Aims
Develop a machine learning algorithm using a national electronic health records database to identify sequences of healthcare utilization, prescription medications, and diagnostic codes that are probabilistically indicative of prodromal bipolar disorder in adolescents and young adults. These sequences will be taxonomized in collaboration with community members and psychiatrists, and a case-control study will estimate each sequence’s relative risk for future suicide attempt and death by suicide.

Under Review

Quantifying Multi-Agent Re-identification Risk in Open and Public Datasets2027-2029
Role
Multiple Principal Investigator (with Stephen Hutt and Sari Reisner)
Funder
Schmidt Sciences, in collaboration with Google DeepMind, the Cooperative AI Foundation, the Advanced Research and Invention Agency (ARIA), and Google.org
Program
Scaling AI Safety for a Multi-Agent World
Total Costs
$300,000
Aims
Assess whether cooperative teams of artificial intelligence (AI) agents can re-identify research participants in open and public datasets. Using rarity metadata from existing public health and education datasets, we will generate a synthetic population, then select a target individual and random samples of varying size. Iterative simulation trials across multi-agent structures will quantify the accuracy, precision, and recall of agents’ performance in correctly re-identifying the target or excluding it from the sample. Findings will inform privacy-protective tools for research with human subjects (e.g., a sandbox that strips high-risk identifiers from repository-bound datasets), supporting open science as AI capabilities continue to advance.

In Preparation

Pre-Diagnostic Electronic Health Record Trajectories and Suicide Risk in Adolescents and Young Adults with Emerging Bipolar Disorder
Role
Multiple Principal Investigator (with Sari Reisner)
Funder
National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), National Institutes of Health (NIH)
Mechanism
Research Project Grant (R01)
Aims
Identify pre-diagnostic electronic health record (EHR) event sequences preceding bipolar disorder, organize these sequences into a clinically interpretable taxonomy informed by patients and clinicians, and quantify sequence-specific risks of suicide attempt and death by suicide among adolescents and young adults, using national EHR data with external validation in a health system cohort.
